# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

Spark serves two overlapping kinds of users in the same session, without a hard split between them: people killing a spare few minutes who want a low-effort, scroll-driven dopamine hit, and people who came with genuine intent to learn something. The app doesn't segment these into different modes or onboarding paths — the same infinite, personalized, one-fact-per-card feed satisfies both, and the Dive Deeper chat is there for whichever moment a given user wants to go from passive scrolling to active curiosity.

## Product Purpose

Spark is a mobile-first feed of fun facts — one fact per full-screen card, swiped through TikTok/Reels-style, scaling to thousands or millions of facts across many categories. Content is short-form and immediately legible: one crisp sentence, no hedging or textbook explanation. Personalization (via likes, shares, and dwell time) tilts what surfaces next without ever fully filtering out other categories, so discovery of unexpected topics stays part of the experience.

## Positioning

The differentiator is the feed format itself, not the facts' novelty — a general trivia app or Wikipedia's random-article button hands you static content; Spark is built around the same scroll mechanics that make social feeds habit-forming (frictionless continuation, personalization, one-thing-at-a-time focus), applied to bite-sized learning instead of arbitrary content.

## Brand Personality

Refined, calm, curious. Quiet confidence over gamified energy — the fact is the star, not the chrome around it. The existing "Cool Modern" system (soft lavender/cream palette, Georgia serif headlines + Manrope sans body, pill radii, diffused shadows, no heavy outlines) is the visual expression of this: minimal contrast, generous whitespace, nothing shouting for attention.

Where Spark should still learn from mainstream social apps is mechanics, not tone: the scroll/reward loop that keeps people opening a feed daily (frictionless one-thing-at-a-time pacing, quick personalization signal from lightweight taps, always-more continuation) — borrowed as interaction design, not as the loud/gamified visual language those apps often carry.

## Anti-references

Loud, gamified trivia apps — Duolingo-style heavy gamification, streak-as-pressure mechanics, bright primary colors, mascot characters. Spark should never adopt that register even while borrowing the underlying engagement mechanics from social feeds.

## Design Principles

- **The fact is the only chrome that matters.** Every screen should keep visual competition with the fact text itself to a minimum — actions and navigation stay quiet, small, and out of the way until touched.
- **Borrow the loop, not the loudness.** Take proven feed-hook mechanics (frictionless continuation, lightweight personalization signals, one-thing-at-a-time pacing) from mainstream social apps, but express them through the calm Cool Modern visual system, never through gamified/bright-primary treatments.
- **Breadth over narrow optimization.** Personalization tilts odds, it never fully filters — the catalog's "something for everyone, including things you didn't know you'd love" premise stays intact even as the feed learns preferences.
- **Refined over flashy.** Quiet confidence, minimal contrast, generous whitespace — let color and spacing do the work instead of heavy outlines, bright accents, or busy overlays.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard baseline: solid text/background contrast, reduced-motion support for any animated transitions, no strict formal WCAG level target beyond good practice.
